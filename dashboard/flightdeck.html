WHAT EVERY READOUT MEANS

Two sensors feed this page. The MPU6050 measures acceleration and rotation; the BMP280 measures air pressure, which is converted into altitude. Everything below is derived from those two.

ARTIFICIAL HORIZON

Pitch and roll are computed from which way gravity is pulling: pitch = asin(-ax/|a|), roll = atan2(ay, az). The ladder is marked every 10°.

Because it works off gravity alone, it is only trustworthy when the board is roughly still. Shake it and the horizon lurches — that is real acceleration being mistaken for gravity, not a bug.

It also cannot show heading. Yaw needs a magnetometer, which this build does not have.

TOTAL G-FORCE

|a| = √(ax² + ay² + az²) — the strength of all acceleration combined, gravity included.

Sitting on a desk it should read 1.00. Freefall reads 0. The box turns red outside 0.40–1.80 g.

Yours reads about 1.08 at rest — a scale error in the sensor, not motion. Subtract that offset before trusting small readings.

ALTITUDE

Barometric, not GPS. The firmware averages 50 pressure samples at boot and calls that zero, then converts every later reading into a height difference from it.

Shown relative to your last TARE, in centimetres. Press TARE ALT to re-zero at the board's current height — the useful way to measure a small lift.

σ is the noise in the last second of samples: the real resolution. If σ is 4 cm, a 2 cm movement is not measurable. Raising DAMP lowers σ.

Pressure drifts as the sensor warms, so altitude creeps for the first few minutes. Let the temperature settle, then TARE.

ROTATION RATE

The gyroscope, as √(gx² + gy² + gz²) in degrees per second. Green under 150, amber to 240, red beyond.

This is rate of turn, not angle. Hold the board at a steep tilt and it reads zero, because it is not moving.

At rest yours shows roughly 2°/s of bias rather than a true zero. Harmless here, but it would accumulate if you ever integrated it into an angle.

VERT RATE & DAMPING

VERT RATE is how fast altitude is changing, fitted across the last second. Positive is upward.

DAMP sets the smoothing on both the horizon and the altitude. LIVE reacts instantly and jitters; CALM is steady but lags about half a second. STEADY is the middle.

Use CALM for measuring a height, LIVE for watching motion.

STREAM & TILES

The graph holds the last 420 samples — about 21 seconds at 20 Hz.

PRESSURE in pascals is the raw barometer reading; altitude is derived from it. TEMP is the BMP280's own die, so it reads above room temperature.

SAMPLES/s should sit near 20. If it sags, the link is dropping lines.
